The Woman in the Wilderness and the Dispute in Heaven

Correcting a single Greek verb in Revelation 12 and the false “war in heaven” it created.

Excerpt from The End of Time · by Raphael Asanti · Revelation 12 · Greek text, mistranslation, and spiritual dispute.

Concept in brief

This excerpt explains how the “war in heaven” in Revelation 12 is not a physical battle, but a spiritual dispute created by a mistranslation of the Greek verb describing the child of the woman in the wilderness.

“The Greek verb does not mean ‘rule.’ It means shepherd – to guide, to care for, not to dominate.”

When translators replaced shepherd with rule, later interpreters assumed the child was destined to govern the nations with force. That single change produced an entire doctrinal structure that does not exist in the Greek text and made the woman’s child appear to be a ruler instead of a shepherd.

Two false spiritual positions

The mistranslation created two opposing, but equally incorrect, spiritual impulses that your excerpt names explicitly:

  • the bull of Baal – the impulse to rule over people
  • the plow – the impulse to destroy the world and begin again

“Neither position is correct. Neither reflects the Greek text. Both are consequences of a single mistranslated line.”

The Greek word polemos in this context does not describe a military battle. It describes a dispute: a spiritual conflict over meaning and intent. One faction believed the child was meant to rule; another believed the world should be destroyed and remade. Both conclusions arise from the mistranslated verb, not from the original text.

The woman in the wilderness

The Greek term used for the woman’s location is erēmos — solitary, isolated, protected. Your excerpt emphasizes that she is taken to be completely alone and cared for directly by God.

“She is taken to be completely alone – the word is eremos, meaning solitary, isolated – and she is cared for directly by God Almighty.”

She is not present in this universe and does not participate in the dispute. The English translation makes it appear as if her flight into the wilderness triggers a war in heaven, but the Greek text simply places the scenes side by side, without causal linkage.

Narrative coherence once the mistranslation is removed

When the incorrect verb is removed, the narrative of Revelation becomes coherent. The woman is isolated and protected; the dispute in heaven concerns the meaning of the child’s role, not a physical war triggered by her actions.

“The English wording creates a dramatic chain reaction that the original text never intended, which is why the mistranslation feels deliberate rather than accidental.”

Understanding this spiritual dispute clarifies the rest of Revelation. The remaining symbols fall back into their original pattern, and the final vision can be read without the false choices introduced by the mistranslation.
