The Woman in the Wilderness and the Dispute in Heaven

Correcting a single Greek verb in Revelation 12 and the false “war in heaven” it created.

Concept in Brief

This excerpt explains how the “war in heaven” in Revelation 12 is not a physical battle, but a spiritual dispute created by a mistranslation of the Greek verb describing the child of the woman in the wilderness.

“The Greek verb does not mean ‘rule.’ It means shepherd – to guide, to care for, not to dominate.”

When translators replaced shepherd with rule, interpreters assumed the child was destined to govern the nations with force. That single change produced an entire doctrinal structure that does not exist in the Greek text.

Two False Spiritual Positions

  • [the bull of Baal] — the impulse to rule over people
  • [the plow] — the impulse to destroy the world and begin again

Neither position is correct. Both arise from the mistranslated verb, not from the original Greek. The word polemos describes a dispute — a conflict over meaning — not a military battle.

The Woman in the Wilderness

The Greek term for her location is erēmos — solitary, isolated, protected. She is taken to be completely alone and cared for directly by God.

She is not present in this universe and does not participate in the dispute. English translations make it appear as if her flight triggers a war in heaven, but the Greek text simply places the scenes side by side.

Narrative Coherence

Once the mistranslation is removed, the narrative becomes coherent. The dispute concerns the meaning of the child’s role, not a physical war triggered by the woman.

“The English wording creates a dramatic chain reaction that the original text never intended.”
